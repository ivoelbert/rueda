# Rueda

An arcade rueda de casino game.

## Guapea and the moves library (current)

`guapea/` is an isometric pixel rueda that dances the guapea in unison, tuned for feel rather than accuracy. The root page opens it.

The moves library on the left lists the calls. Pick one and press Space (or tap the game): the whole rueda dances it from the next 1, then goes back to the guapea (or, after Arriba, keeps walking arriba until the dile que no). As in a real rueda, call on 5: a move prepares on the 7 and 8 before its 1, so a call made on 7 or 8 waits for the 1 after. A second call can be queued while a move is running; during a longer move it waits for the move to end. The list shows only the moves that can be called next, under "Guapea – available moves" or "Arriba – available moves". From the guapea there are fifteen calls, and from arriba three:

- **Dame**: a dile que no with the girl on your right. On the 7 and 8 before it, the leads and the girls on their right turn about a quarter round toward each other and drift away from their partners, still holding hands. On 1 every lead lets go, turns to face the center and steps in, and the girl on his right turns half round to her left into his arms: his right hand on her back, her left arm up and then over onto his back by the 3. On 5 his left hand takes her right and she crosses in front of him, turning on 6 to face him, so everyone is home by 7. The leads keep their places and the girls move on one place, so everyone has a new partner.
- **Enchufla**: two bars, the enchufla and then a dame. On the 7 and 8 before it, the joined hands come up between each couple. On 1 the lead breaks back and lifts her right hand over her head; on 2-3 she walks across in front of him, turning half round to her left under his arm, while he passes behind her turning half round to his right, so by 4 they have swapped places and are already starting back. On 5 they let go and walk back past each other, him on the inside, each turning to face the new partner (the girl on his right) as they come together. They keep looking at each other and keep moving through 8, flowing straight into the dame's 1. The second bar is that dame, so everyone has a new partner. Since the leads keep their places, he walks back to his spot instead of on around the circle.
- **Enchufla doble**: three bars, a half enchufla and then a whole enchufla. It starts like the enchufla: on 2-3-4 she walks forward turning to her left under his arm, without a pause on 4. They pass each other as in the enchufla, and on 5, once they have crossed and he is beyond her at her left side, his right hand on her back stops her partway round. They turn their heads to look at each other while she throws her left arm up, out in front of her so it clears his head. On 5-6-7 he sends her back under his arm, spinning her to her right a little past facing him, and they keep moving through 8 to be home on the next 1, hands up, for the enchufla (with its dame).
- **Sombrero**: four bars with both hands held and crossed: his right holds her right, and his left holds her left. On the 7 and 8 before it he takes both her hands, his right on top. First bar, an exhíbela: on 1 they open out a little and on 2-3 they come back in lifting both hands up over her head, so on 5-6-7 she turns once round to her right under them, which crosses them the other way, his left on top. Second bar, an enchufla without letting go: on 1 the hands go up over her head, and on 2-3 they pass right shoulder to right shoulder, she on the inside turning to her left under the hands. They finish it on 4-5 a step apart, swapped, her on his right, turned toward each other halfway between facing the center and facing each other, the joined hands down low between them. On 5-6 they step back in lifting the hands over their heads, and on 7 comes the coronación: his left hand takes her left over his head and his right takes her right over hers, so each wears the other's arm as a hat. On 8 they let go of both hands: his right arm round her and her left round him slide down onto each other's backs, which is the dile que no's hold, and the outside hands come down free. Third bar, the dile que no from that hold, so nobody's arm goes up as she turns to face him on 1-2-3. On 5-6-7 she crosses in front of him back to her side, his left hand holding her right, handing her on to the next guy. Fourth bar, the dame: everyone has a new partner.
- **Setenta**: five bars. On the 7 and 8 before it he takes both her hands, straight: his left holds her right, and his right her left. First bar, an exhíbela that locks her arm: on 1-2-3 his left hand takes her right up over her head while his right keeps hers low at the waist, and on 5-6-7 she turns once round to her right under the high hands. The low hands go round her as she turns, so her left arm ends wrapped behind her back, his right hand holding it there round her right side; he faces the same way throughout. Then three swaps of places in a row, one per half bar, so the couple goes round each other like gears, with no stop on 4 or 8. Second bar, an enchufla without letting go: on 1-2-3 they ease a little apart, and on 5-6-7 his left hand takes her right over her head and she turns half round to her left on the inside while he walks round her on the outside, which unwinds her arm, so they finish swapped holding both hands in front. Third bar: on 1-2-3 it is his enchufla, letting go with his left, taking her left hand up over his own head with his right and turning half round to his left under it on the inside while she walks round him; on 4 his left takes her right again. On 5-6-7 she does the last enchufla: he lets go with his right and she turns half round to her left under his left hand, so they finish swapped. Fourth bar, a dile que no from there, his left hand keeping hers, handing her on to the next guy. Fifth bar, the dame: everyone has a new partner.
- **Setenta corrido**: six bars, the setenta with one more bar after the first. Once her left arm is wrapped behind her back, face to face, the couple walks once round the middle between them, clockwise (to their right), still wrapped and with the hands as they are, stepping on every count. It flows on from her turn, eases in on 1, goes round at an even pace through the 4 and eases out by the 8, back where it started, him facing the same way as before. Then the rest of the setenta: the enchufla without letting go, his enchufla and hers, the dile que no and the dame. Everyone has a new partner.
- **Uno**: four bars with both hands held like a handshake, his right in her right and his left in her left, while he dances behind her and they swap sides like a windshield wiper. On whichever side he is, her arm on that side is wrapped behind her back, held there by his hand. First bar: on 1 his right hand takes her right and she walks past him on the inside, right shoulder to right shoulder; on 2-3 she turns a quarter to her left to face the center and he steps in behind her on her left, turning a quarter to his right, so her right arm wraps behind her back, and his left takes her left. On 5-6-7 they swap sides: she slides to her left in front of him and he to his right behind her, so her right arm comes out and her left wraps behind her back. Second bar: on 1-2-3 they swap back. On 5 his right hand brings her right arm out by her side and up over her head, and on 5-6-7 he steps up beside her on her left, both turning toward each other, while her left hand goes up over his head: the coronación on 7, as in the sombrero. On 8 they let go into the dile que no's hold. Third bar, the sombrero's dile que no, handing her on to the next guy; fourth bar, the dame: everyone has a new partner.
- **Pelota uno, Pelota dos, Pelota tres**: an enchufla (with its dame) where everyone claps on the way to the new partner: on 7 for the pelota uno, on 7 and 8 for the pelota dos, and on 7, 8 and the dame's 1 for the pelota tres. The hands come up in front of the chin a little apart before the first clap, meet right on each clapped count with a spark and a clap sound, and go back to the enchufla or the dame after the last; in the pelota tres her arm goes up for the dame right after the clap on 1. Everyone has a new partner.
- **Festival pelota**: six bars, the pelota uno, dos and tres back to back, so one clap, then two, then three, with a new partner each time.
- **Pelota**: five bars, her right hand held all the way, and the couple keeps going round each other the same way, flowing through the 4s and 8s. First bar, an enchufla without letting go, and on 5-6-7 they carry on a quarter round, so he ends a step inside the circle facing out and she a step outside facing in; on 6-7 his right takes her right from his left. On 8-1 comes the golpe: both turn to their left, past side by side, stepping a little apart, so they look opposite ways with the joined right arms stretched between them; his free arm stretches out to the center and hers away from it, and their heads follow their hands. On 2-3 they come back to face each other, and on 5-6-7 she turns once to her right while he turns once to his left, his left taking her right as they pass back to back. Third bar, an enchufla back and a quarter further, so they're in line with the circle again, swapped, as at the end of the setenta. Fourth bar, the setenta's dile que no hands her on; fifth bar, the dame: everyone has a new partner.
- **Doble play con mambo**: three bars. First bar, the enchufla, except that he comes to rest on 8 facing the next girl instead of walking on into the dame. Second bar, the mambo, a shine where everyone dances the same feet: on 1 the left foot taps a little forward on its toes, on 2 it comes back, on 3 the right foot taps and on 4 it comes back, the hips going over the standing foot, and the hand on the other side reaches a little forward with each tap. On 5-6-7-8 the feet go on and everyone claps on 5 and 7. Third bar, the dame with the girl he did the mambo with: everyone has a new partner.
- **Pelota de 4**: four bars, like the doble play con mambo. First bar, the enchufla, resting on 8 facing the next girl. Second bar, a whole bar of mambo: the left foot taps on 1 and 5, the right on 3 and 7, the hand on the other side reaching forward with each tap. Third bar, everyone turns once round to the left in place, in four quarter turns that snap round just before 1, 3, 5 and 7 and land with a clap on the count, the left foot stepping round with the turn and the right closing after it, so on 7 they face each other again. Fourth bar, the dame with that same girl: everyone has a new partner.
- **Arriba**: a call that changes what the rueda does between moves. First bar, a dile que si: on 1-2-3 a short extension, leaning away from each other with the joined hands down and out, and on 5-6-7 the couple turns half round together to his right, pivoting round the middle between them (he goes round the outside of the circle, she round the inside), into the closed hold: his right hand on her back, her left on his shoulder, his left holding her right at about head height. They end swapped, in line with the circle, him facing South, and from 5 on they are already setting off along the circle. Meanwhile the rueda closes up: from 2 into the middle of the next bar the couples drift in toward the center until the circle is about 0.72 of its size (less with three or four couples, so the guys going round inside have room), so a place round the circle is about 31 px instead of 43 and the walk and the tarritos cover less ground. Partners stay the same distance apart while it does. From the next bar on every couple walks arriba, bar after bar: he walks forward and she backward, stepping on 1-2-3 and 5-6-7, his left foot first and her right, one place round the circle a bar at an even pace, gliding on through the 4 and the 8. The joined hands pump, up on 1, down on 2, up on 3, down on 5, up on 6, down on 7, each one's shoulder on that side going up with them. Everyone keeps their partner. They keep walking until the next call, and from arriba the dile que no, the tarrito, the tarrito hasta la tuya, adentro y afuera or la exhibo can be called.
- **Dile que no** (from arriba or adentro y afuera): one bar out of the closed hold. On 1-2-3 he rocks back, turning a little to his left, and on 5-6-7 she crosses in front of him while he turns half round to his left, so they are back in the guapea with the same partner, at the spot they walked to. The rueda opens back out to its full size as they do it, over the bar and the first half of the next one.
- **Tarrito** (from arriba): one bar in which everyone changes partner and keeps walking. On 1 they let go of each other's backs and the joined hands go up into an arch, both stretching up. He hurries on under it, past her on the inside of the circle, right shoulder by right shoulder, while she keeps walking backward at the arriba pace. He still steps on 1-2-3 and 5-6-7, but with long strides: each foot stays down only until the other lands, so the foot behind takes one long stride through the 4 and the 8 while he keeps going. His free hands swing as he walks, the one on the other side going forward and a little up with each step, as in the mambo. They let go on 4 and he walks on to the girl ahead, whose partner has just gone under her arm too: on 6 his left hand takes her right, and by the 8 they are in the closed hold, walking on arriba. He covers two places in the bar and she one, so everyone has a new partner.
- **Tarrito hasta la tuya** (from arriba): the tarrito, but he doesn't take the girl ahead. He walks on round the inside of the circle, at the tarrito's pace and stepping the same way, a little further in than in the tarrito, passing the girls on their inside at an even pace, about the tarrito's, while the girls keep walking arriba, until he comes back to his own girl: in the last bar he comes out to her and takes her into the closed hold by the 8, as in the tarrito, and they walk on arriba. It takes about a bar for every 1.6 couples (five bars with eight couples), and everyone ends with the partner they started with.
- **Adentro y afuera** (from arriba): another call that changes what the rueda does between moves. On the 1 the couples stop walking and, still in the closed hold, open and close like a V, bar after bar until the next call. On 1 he side-steps in, his left foot toward the center, turning about 20° toward her, while she turns a quarter to her right to face the center and steps back out with her right foot; they rock on 2 and meet in the middle on 3. On 5 the same the other way: he side-steps out with his right and she turns to face out and steps back in with her left; they meet again on 7. It flows on through the 4 and the 8 with no pause. The joined hands are at about shoulder height while he is in and head height while he is out. Everyone keeps their partner. From adentro y afuera the dile que no, arriba (they walk on from the middle), the tarrito or la exhibo can be called, and the call has to come by the 5, since from the 7 the couples head either in again or to the middle for the next move; a later call waits for the bar after.
- **Tarrito** (from adentro y afuera): the tarrito's arch, and he walks under her arm past her to the next girl, but the girls wait where they are, stepping in place, so he walks just one place. By the 8 he has the new girl in the closed hold and they go on in and out. Everyone has a new partner.
- **La exhibo** (from adentro y afuera, or from arriba, where they stop walking for it): on 1-2-3 the in and out of adentro y afuera, but she stays facing the center instead of turning back to him, and on 4 walks on forward into it. On 5, as he steps out, he lifts the joined hands (his left, the inside one) over her head, she reaches out to the center with her free left arm, and on 5-6-7 she turns once round to her right under his arm. They let go of each other's backs for the turn and are back in the closed hold by the 8, her still facing the center, and they go on in and out. Everyone keeps their partner.

Moves live in `MOVES` in `guapea/main.js`. A move is one or more bars of 8 counts, and each bar is a figure that gives the lead and the follow a root path around the circle (places traveled, distance from the center, turn; the first bar can start at -2, on the 7 before the move), pelvis and footstep keys per count, and how many places the girls move on (`shift`). The hands for each figure are scripted in `hands()`.

- Keys: Space = call the selected move, ▲ ▼ or 1-9 and 0 = pick one of the moves listed (0 is the tenth; from the eleventh on, ▲ ▼ or a click), ◄ ► = lead/follow, S = ½× speed, M = mute, T = the tune panel.
- The tune panel's sliders (tempo, bounce, straight legs, hips, step size, arm swing, styling, timing, springiness, looseness, couples) are saved in the URL, so a tuned version can be shared as a link.
- URL options: `?beat=N` freezes the clock at a beat (count 1 is beat 0), `?call=dame` queues that move from the second measure (beat 8 on; the enchufla and the pelotas run to beat 24, `?call=doble` and `?call=dobleplay` to beat 32, `?call=sombrero`, `?call=uno` and `?call=pelota4` to beat 40, `?call=setenta` and `?call=pelota` to beat 48, and `?call=setentacorrido` and `?call=festival` to beat 56; several calls go in a list, each from the given measure or right after the one before, so `?call=arriba,dilequeno@4` walks arriba on beats 16-31 and the dile que no runs to beat 40, `?call=arriba,tarrito@3,dilequeno@5` has the tarrito on beats 24-31, `?call=arriba,tuya@3,dilequeno@8` has the tarrito hasta la tuya on beats 24-63 with eight couples, and `?call=arriba,adentro@3,tarrito2@5,dilequeno@7` dances adentro y afuera from beat 24, its tarrito on beats 40-47 and the dile que no on beats 56-63; `?call=arriba,adentro@3,exhibo@5,dilequeno@7` has la exhibo on beats 32-39; `arriba2` is arriba out of adentro y afuera, and `exhibo2` la exhibo out of arriba), `?play=1` skips the title, `?role=follow`, `?move=enchufla` selects a move.

## Tools

`tools/` has checks for working on the moves. They run the game in a headless Chromium with Playwright, so run `npm install` and `npx playwright install chromium` once first.

- `npm run check` dances every move in the library, each from the guapea, arriba or adentro y afuera as it needs, and reports per move: the biggest jump from one 0.05-beat step to the next of any pelvis, hand or heading (a snap), how far planted feet slide, how close any two dancers come, and anything the page throws. A number past the limits at the top of `tools/check.mjs` is marked with `!` and the command fails; the limits sit just above the worst the moves had when the tools were added, so they catch a move getting worse.
  - `npm run check -- --against HEAD` also dances them at that commit and says which moves now dance differently and from which beat, so a change can be seen to touch only the moves it should.
  - `--only setenta,uno` checks just those moves; `--query '&stand=1'` adds URL options.
- `npm run strip -- out.png setentacorrido 2 15 16 17 18` draws couple 2 (lead 2 and the girl nearest him) alone at those beats, side by side, labeled with the counts. The calls are what `?call=` takes (`''` for the guapea); `--all` draws everyone.

For them the game exposes `window.rueda`, with `pose(beat)` to pose everyone without drawing, `only(f)` to draw just the dancers `f` picks, and `moves`, `leads()`, `follows()`, `seek(beat)` and `proj`.

## Coin-op cabinets

- `arcade83/`: an early-80s vertical maze board (224x288). Top-down rueda, Namco-style wavetable band.
- `arcade86/`: a mid-80s sports coin-op (256x224). Wooden stage, bleachers, lamp scoreboard, NES-style band.
- `arcade92/`: an early-90s fighter (384x224). Side-on plaza, SABOR bars, 99 timer, FM band.

Each one runs the full loop: attract mode (title and demo pages), insert coin, pick lead or follow, a 99-second round, then game over. The hi-score is saved in localStorage.

Keys: Space/Enter/Z = the big ¡ENCHUFLA DOBLE! button, 5/C = coin, arrows or A/D = lead/follow, S = ½× práctica.

## Shared code

- `shared/dance.js`: the choreography (guapea, media enchufla, enchufla, dile que no) as keyframes per count, footwork, and the IK skeleton every version draws.
- `shared/game.js`: beat clock, call queue, timing judgment and score. The earlier prototypes also use its HTML HUD.
- `shared/audio.js`: synthesized casino band (clave, campana, congas, bass, piano) and sound effects.
- `shared/chip.js`: the same band on three sound chips (namco, nes, fm), plus a brass hook and coin-op sound effects.
- `shared/arcade.js`, `shared/arcade.css`: the cabinet (marquee, CRT, bezel cards, control panel) and the attract → select → play → over loop.
- `shared/sprite.js`: low-res sprite drawing snapped to a palette. `shared/font.js`: the 5x7 bitmap font.

## Running

Run `python3 serve.py 8000` and open http://localhost:8000/. The site is plain static files, deployed to GitHub Pages from `main`.

URL options:
- `?beat=18` freezes the clock at a beat. The enchufla doble runs from beat 8 to beat 32.
- `?role=follow`
- Cabinets only: `?state=attract|select|ready|play|over`, `?page=title|demo`, `?heat=0..1`.
- Earlier prototypes only: `?cam=close`, `?autoplay`.
